# FOIA Request Processing Guidance (Training Excerpt)

*This is a simplified, synthetic training document modeled on public federal FOIA guidance. Use for course labs only.*

## Overview

The Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) gives any person the right to request access to federal agency records. Agencies must respond to a FOIA request within 20 business days of receipt, though this period may be extended in unusual circumstances.

## Intake

When a request arrives, the FOIA officer logs it with a tracking number, records the date of receipt, and sends the requester an acknowledgment letter containing the tracking number. Requests may arrive by mail, email, or the agency's online portal.

## Determining Fees

Requesters fall into fee categories: commercial, educational/noncommercial scientific, news media, and "all other." The category determines whether search, review, and duplication fees apply. The first two hours of search time and the first 100 pages of duplication are provided at no charge to non-commercial requesters.

## Searching for Records

The officer identifies which program offices are likely to hold responsive records and issues a search tasking. Program offices must conduct a reasonable search of systems likely to contain the records and return them within the internal deadline.

## Applying Exemptions

FOIA contains nine exemptions. The most frequently applied are Exemption 5 (deliberative process privilege), Exemption 6 (personal privacy), and Exemption 7 (law enforcement records). Personally identifiable information such as Social Security numbers is withheld under Exemption 6 and redacted before release.

## Response

The officer issues a determination letter stating whether the request is granted in full, granted in part, or denied, and describes the requester's right to appeal. Released records are provided in the requester's preferred format where practicable.
